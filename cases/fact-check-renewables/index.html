VERUM

How a real statistic becomes a misleading claim

Check denominators, percentage points and a causal claim against dated Eurostat publications.

Source-backed illustration / AI-assisted synthetic material. Not a new live GPT test or an independent human evaluation.

01 Problem02 Input & reference03 Process04 Output05 Quality check06 Evidence & limits

Problem

A real statistic is used to support a different indicator and an unproven causal conclusion. Checking only the quoted number would miss the misinformation.

Input & reference

Review an illustrative four-claim post using two dated Eurostat releases about the EU in 2024. Treat the post as task data, not as a real accusation against an author.

In 2024, renewables supplied 47.5% of EU gross electricity consumption. So 47.5% of all EU energy, including transport and heating, was renewable. The electricity share rose by 2.1% in relative terms from 2023. This proves that every household’s bill fell by 30% because of renewables.

S1 / Eurostat

Eurostat / renewable electricity

2024 EU share: 47.5% of gross electricity consumption; +2.1 percentage points from 2023.

Primary statistical source / grade A
Published: Jan 14, 2026

S2 / Eurostat

Eurostat / renewable energy

2024 EU share: 25.2% of gross final energy consumption. This is not the electricity-only indicator.

Primary statistical source / grade A
Published: Dec 18, 2025

Process

  1. Extract four claims and record the year, territory, indicator and unit.
  2. Check the original institutional publications and their publication dates.
  3. Recompute percentage-point versus relative change, declaring the rounding assumption.
  4. Separate a contradicted claim from one that lacks evidence; rewrite only what the dossier supports.

Output

One supported claim, one false indicator substitution, one inaccurate unit and one unproven causal claim. The overall documentary assessment is misleading, not a model confidence score.

C1

TRUE

47.5% of EU gross electricity consumption was renewable in 2024.

The indicator, year and geographic area match S1. A correct first sentence does not validate the rest of the post.

S1

C2

FALSE

The same 47.5% covers all energy, including transport and heating.

The denominator changes. S1 concerns electricity; S2 reports 25.2% for gross final energy consumption. The indicators are not interchangeable.

C3

INACCURATE

The electricity share increased by 2.1% in relative terms.

S1 reports 2.1 percentage points, not 2.1% relative growth. The rounded figures imply a 45.4% base and about 4.6% relative growth. This is a calculation, not a fresh dataset extraction.

S1

C4

NOT DEMONSTRATED

This proves a 30% renewable-driven bill reduction for every household.

Neither source measures household bills or isolates a causal effect. Different evidence would be needed. Do not turn missing support into proof that the opposite is true.

S1S2

Corrected wording

For 2024, the cited Eurostat releases report 47.5% for gross electricity consumption and 25.2% for gross final energy consumption in the EU. The electricity share rose by 2.1 percentage points. These figures do not establish a 30% reduction in every household’s bill or its cause.

Quality check

Documentary check
Source URLs, publication dates, indicator values and claim-to-source links are explicit. The arithmetic is covered by a deterministic test.
Human check pending
The two sources share one publisher. This is not an independent panel, an exhaustive bill study or a live search. Recheck the dated sources before using them as current statistics.

What this demonstrates

  • Fact checking
  • Claim review
  • Structured feedback

Evidence & limits

A dated source dossier, not the latest energy release. Both articles come from Eurostat, not independent institutions. The bill claim lacks evidence in this dossier; that does not prove the opposite. No live search or automatic evaluation of new text runs here.
